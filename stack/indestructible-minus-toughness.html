Indestructible
vs
-X/-X

What Indestructible Does Not Stop

Indestructible only stops two things: effects that use the word destroy, and lethal damage. It does nothing against effects that put a creature into the graveyard some other way. Reducing toughness to 0 or less, sacrifice, and exile all get through, because none of them destroy the creature.

Two Separate State-Based Actions

Indestructible ignores the lethal-damage check, not the zero-toughness check. A 10/10 indestructible with 12 damage marked on it survives, because the lethal-damage rule is the one indestructible turns off. Drop its toughness to 0 or less and a different rule applies, one indestructible never touches.

✓ How It Works

Indestructible answers exactly one question: can this be destroyed? Destruction comes only from a "destroy" effect or from the lethal-damage and deathtouch state-based actions. A creature with 0 or less toughness is handled by its own rule and is put into the graveyard without being destroyed, so indestructible has nothing to say about it. Sacrifice and exile move the creature without destroying it either.

✕ Common Misconception

Indestructible is not blanket protection. It does not stop -X/-X effects like Toxic Deluge, -1/-1 counters, sacrifice effects, exile, or bounce. Damage is also a trap for the eye: it never lowers toughness, so a pile of marked damage on an indestructible creature does nothing on its own.

Technical Breakdown

Why The Negative Effects Get Through

  • Indestructible is narrow. A permanent with indestructible cannot be destroyed and is not destroyed by lethal damage. It ignores the state-based action that checks for lethal damage. That is the whole of what it does. (CR 700.4, 702.12b)
  • "Destroyed" has a strict definition. The only ways a permanent is destroyed are an effect that uses the word destroy, the lethal-damage check, or the deathtouch check. Anything else that sends a permanent to the graveyard has not destroyed it. (CR 701.8b)
  • Zero toughness is a different rule. A creature with toughness 0 or less is put into its owner's graveyard by its own state-based action. This is not destruction, so indestructible does not apply and regeneration cannot replace it. (CR 704.5f)
  • -X/-X reduces toughness for real. Effects like Toxic Deluge and -1/-1 counters lower the creature's actual toughness. Take it to 0 or less and the zero-toughness rule removes it, regardless of indestructible. (CR 704.5f)
  • Damage never lowers toughness. Damage is only marked on the creature and stays there until the cleanup step. A 10/10 dealt 12 damage is still a 10/10 with 12 damage marked, not a creature with reduced toughness. (CR 120.3e, 120.6)
  • Sacrifice and exile also dodge it. Sacrifice moves the creature to the graveyard without destroying it, and exile sends it to a different zone entirely. An indestructible permanent can still be sacrificed, exiled, or bounced to hand. (CR 700.4, 701.8b)

Worked Scenarios

The first shows that damage and -X/-X are not the same thing. The second shows the line that actually kills an indestructible creature.

Eye Gouge On A Damaged 10/10

Your 10/10 indestructible has been dealt 12 damage this turn. The lethal-damage check would normally destroy it, but indestructible ignores that check, so it survives with 12 damage marked. Order does not matter here. Your opponent then casts Eye Gouge for -1/-1. The creature becomes a 9/9 with 12 damage still marked. Its toughness is 9, which is above 0, so the zero-toughness rule does not fire and the lethal-damage rule is still ignored. The creature lives. Marked damage does not stack onto a toughness reduction, because damage and toughness are tracked separately. (CR 120.6, 704.5f, 704.5g)

Toxic Deluge Or A Big -X/-X

The same 10/10 indestructible is on the battlefield. Your opponent casts Toxic Deluge for X equal to 10, giving every creature -10/-10. Your creature is now a 0/0. The zero-toughness state-based action puts it into the graveyard, and indestructible offers no protection because this is not destruction. The same outcome follows from any effect that drives toughness to 0 or less, such as a morbid Tragic Slip or enough -1/-1 counters. Forcing a sacrifice or exiling the creature with Swords to Plowshares removes it just as cleanly, since neither destroys it. (CR 704.5f, 701.8b, 700.4)
