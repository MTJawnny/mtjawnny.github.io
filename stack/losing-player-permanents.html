When a Player Leaves

Control, Theft & Elimination

When a player loses or leaves a multiplayer game, every card and permanent they own leaves with them. Permanents they controlled but did not own behave differently depending on how they got control. A stolen permanent reverts to its previous controller, while a permanent reanimated or pulled from another player's graveyard or library is exiled. Commanders follow these same rules, then their owner may send them to the command zone.

Where Each Permanent Goes When They Leave

Sort every permanent the leaving player controlled by how they got it. What they own leaves with them. What they stole snaps back. What they pulled from a graveyard or library is exiled.

✓ How It Works

Look at how the leaving player got each permanent. If they own it, it leaves the game. If they took it with a lasting control effect like Control Magic, control snaps back to whoever held it before. If they put it onto the battlefield from a graveyard or library, no control effect is involved, so it is exiled.

✕ Common Misconception

An opponent leaving does not hand you their board, and it does not destroy everything they controlled. Stolen cards go back to their owners rather than dying, and the creatures they reanimated out of your graveyard are exiled, not given to you.

Technical Breakdown

Sorting the Permanents

  • Owned cards leave. Every object the departing player owns is removed from the game, in every zone. This is not destruction or exile; the cards are simply gone. (CR 800.4a)
  • Control effects end. Any effect that gave that player control of an object ends. A creature taken with an aura or a lasting "gain control" effect reverts to whoever controlled it before. (CR 800.4a)
  • Reanimated and library-stolen cards are exiled. A permanent they put onto the battlefield from a graveyard or library was never under a control effect, so after they leave it is still controlled by them and is exiled. (CR 800.4a)
  • Stack objects vanish. Spells and abilities they controlled that are not represented by cards cease to exist, such as a copy on the stack or an activated ability. (CR 800.4a)
  • It happens at once. All of this resolves the instant the player leaves, not as a state-based action, so it finishes before anyone receives priority. (CR 800.4a)

Borrowed Permanents and Departed Controllers

  • Borrowed now, exiled later. If a player still in the game is holding that permanent through their own control effect, it stays for now. Once that effect ends, the permanent is exiled, because the player it would return to has left. (CR 800.4c)
  • Nothing moves to a departed player. Control cannot pass to someone who has left. Pending control changes, tokens, and triggered abilities that would be theirs simply do not happen. (CR 800.4b, 800.4d)
  • The default controller matters. A permanent's home controller is the player it entered under. When that player is gone, there is no one for it to revert to, which is why it is exiled rather than handed off. (CR 110.2)

Commanders

  • Their own commander leaves with them. A player's commander is owned by that player, so it is removed from the game on elimination. Leaving the game is not a move to exile or a graveyard, so the command zone option does not apply to it. (CR 800.4a, 903.9a)
  • Your stolen commander comes back. If they held your commander with a control effect, it reverts to you on the battlefield. If they reanimated or cast it from your graveyard or library, it is exiled first, and then you may move it from exile to the command zone. (CR 800.4a, 903.9a)

Worked Scenarios

Three boards that all run on the same rule. The first is the table that prompted this page. The second is the clean teaching pair. The third is the commander payoff.

Geth, Equipment, and a Stolen Creature

You use Geth, Lord of the Vault to pull two Equipment from an opponent's graveyard onto the battlefield under your control, attaching both to Geth. A second opponent then steals Geth with Agent of Treachery, which gains control of Geth only. A third opponent then eliminates you. Geth is owned by you, so it leaves the game, and the player who stole it loses it. The two Equipment are owned by the player whose graveyard they came from, but they entered under your control with no control effect attached, and the Agent of Treachery player never controlled them. So when you leave, the Equipment is still controlled by you, and both are exiled. They do not return to the player who owned them, even though that player is still in the game. (CR 800.4a)

Control Magic vs Bribery

An opponent steals one of your creatures with Control Magic, and uses Bribery to put another of your creatures onto the battlefield straight from your library, both under their control. When they are eliminated, the Control Magic creature reverts to you, because the aura leaves with its owner and the control effect ends. The Bribery creature is exiled, because it entered under their control with no control effect to end. You own both cards, yet only one comes back. The difference is the lasting control effect. (CR 800.4a)

Getting Your Commander Back

An opponent controls your commander and then loses the game. If they took it with a control effect such as Agent of Treachery, it reverts to your control and stays on the battlefield. If instead they reanimated it from your graveyard or cast it from your library, the elimination exiles it, and then a state-based action lets you move it from exile to the command zone. Either way your commander comes back to you; only the route changes. Their own commander, meanwhile, leaves the game with them. (CR 800.4a, 903.9a)
