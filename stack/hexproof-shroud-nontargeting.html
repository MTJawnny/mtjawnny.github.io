Hexproof / Shroud
vs
Non-Targeting

Targeting and Removal Interaction

Hexproof and shroud only stop a permanent from being chosen as a target. They do nothing against effects that never use the word target. Board wipes, sacrifice effects, mass bounce, and vote or choose effects all remove a hexproof or shroud creature the same as any other.

Targeted Removal vs Non-Targeting Removal

The keyword only checks for the word target. A spell that says "destroy target creature" needs a legal target and bounces off. A spell that removes creatures without saying target goes straight through.

✓ How It Works

Hexproof and shroud check one thing: whether a spell or ability uses the word target. Anything that destroys, sacrifices, bounces, exiles, or shrinks creatures without saying target is never stopped. The keyword sits there doing nothing.

✕ Common Misconception

Hexproof and shroud do not make a creature unremovable. They only dodge targeted removal. Board wipes, edicts, mass bounce, and vote or choose effects still get the creature. Reach for one of those instead of single-target removal.

Technical Breakdown

What Counts As Targeting

  • The definition. A spell or ability targets only if its text uses the word target. An instant or sorcery is targeted when it says "target [something]," and Aura spells are always targeted as they are cast. Nothing else targets. (CR 115.1a, 115.1b)
  • Hexproof. A permanent with hexproof can't be the target of spells or abilities your opponents control. Its own controller can still target it. (CR 702.11b)
  • Shroud. A permanent with shroud can't be the target of any spell or ability, including ones its own controller casts. (CR 702.18a)
  • The word target is the test, not "choose" or "all." Words like each, all, choose, and vote show up on plenty of non-targeting effects, but they are not what matters. The only thing that makes a spell targeted is the word target. (CR 115.1)

Effects That Bypass Hexproof and Shroud

  • Board wipes. A spell that destroys, exiles, or shrinks every creature, such as one reading "destroy all creatures" or "all creatures get -X/-X," does not use target, so the keyword never applies. (CR 115.1a)
  • Edicts target the player. A spell reading "target player sacrifices a creature" targets the player, not the creature. The sacrificing player picks which creature, so it only takes a hexproof or shroud creature when that is the one they choose or their only one. (CR 115.1a)
  • Vote and choose effects. An effect that has players vote for or choose a permanent without saying target does not target it, so a hexproof or shroud creature is a legal pick. (CR 115.1)
  • Mass bounce and exile. Returning or exiling every nonland permanent at once does not target any of them, so the keyword gives no protection. (CR 115.1a)

Attaching Auras Without Casting

  • Casting an Aura targets. An Aura you cast targets what it will enchant, so you can't cast one at a creature with shroud, or at an opponent's creature with hexproof. (CR 115.1b)
  • Putting one in without casting does not. An Aura that enters the battlefield by any means other than resolving as a spell does not target. You choose a legal object for it to enchant as it enters, which can be a hexproof or shroud creature. The Aura's enchant restriction still applies, and protection from the Aura's quality still stops it. (CR 303.4f)

Related Keywords

  • Ward is a tax, not a wall. Ward is a triggered ability that fires only when the permanent becomes the target of an opponent's spell or ability, then counters it unless a cost is paid. Non-targeting effects never trigger ward, and ward does not stop you from targeting in the first place. (CR 702.21a)
  • Protection does more. Protection from a quality also stops that quality from enchanting, equipping, blocking, and dealing damage. So unlike hexproof or shroud, it can stop even an Aura attached without casting, and it prevents damage from the quality. (CR 702.16c)

Worked Scenarios

Four boards that all run on the same rule. The keyword only ever checks for the word target, so each of these gets through.

Board Wipe vs a Shroud Creature

Your opponent's creature has shroud, so no one can target it and single-target removal is dead. You cast Toxic Deluge for X equal to its toughness, giving all creatures -X/-X. The spell never says target, so shroud does nothing and the creature dies with the rest of the board. Because it lowers toughness instead of destroying, it also takes down an indestructible creature. (CR 115.1a, 702.18a)

Edict Into a Lone Hexproof Bomb

Your opponent controls one creature, a hexproof threat. Single-target removal can't choose it. You cast Diabolic Edict, which reads "target player sacrifices a creature of their choice." The spell targets the player, not the creature, so hexproof is irrelevant. With only one creature to give up, they have to sacrifice the bomb. If they had a spare creature, they would sacrifice that one instead, so save edicts for when the protected creature is their only one. (CR 115.1a, 702.11b)

Council's Judgment vs Hexproof and Shroud

Council's Judgment has each player vote for a nonland permanent you don't control, then exiles the one with the most votes. Voting is not targeting, so a hexproof or shroud creature is a legal candidate and can be exiled. In a duel both votes fall on your opponent's permanents, so a lone protected creature is exiled outright. Because it exiles rather than dealing damage, it also gets through indestructible. (CR 115.1, 702.18a)

Aura Without Casting (The Cheat)

You can't cast targeted removal at a shroud creature, and you can't cast an Aura at it either, since Aura spells target. You can still attach an Aura by putting it onto the battlefield without casting it, for example a reanimation effect that returns Auras from the graveyard or an attack trigger that searches one out and attaches it. That attachment does not target, so it lands on the shroud creature. The Aura's enchant line still has to allow it, and protection from the Aura's color would still keep it off. (CR 303.4f, 702.16c)
