Ward

Triggered Protection Keyword

Ward is a triggered ability, not a targeting wall. Whenever a permanent with ward becomes the target of a spell or ability an opponent controls, that spell or ability is countered unless the opponent pays the ward cost. It does not stop targeting, it taxes it, and effects that never use the word target slip past it untouched.

Ward {2}: Pay or Get Countered

Ward is a trigger, not a wall. The creature can still be targeted. When an opponent targets it, a ward trigger goes on the stack. They pay the cost or their spell is countered.

✓ How It Works

Ward waits for an opponent to target the permanent, then puts a trigger on the stack. The opponent pays the listed cost, whether mana, life, a discard, or a sacrifice, and their spell resolves. If they don't pay, it is countered. You can always target your own ward permanents for free.

✕ Common Misconception

Ward is not hexproof. The creature can still be targeted, and a determined opponent just pays the cost. Ward also does nothing against board wipes, edicts, or anything that never says target, and an opponent can decline to pay against a spell that can't be countered.

Technical Breakdown

How Ward Resolves

  • What sets it off. Ward triggers only when the permanent becomes the target of a spell or ability an opponent controls. Your own spells and abilities never trigger it. (CR 702.21a)
  • It is a trigger, not a true cost. The ward trigger goes on the stack above the spell that targeted the permanent and resolves first. The opponent pays as that trigger resolves. (CR 603.3)
  • Pay or get countered. If the opponent pays the ward cost, their spell or ability resolves as normal. If they don't, it is countered. (CR 701.5)
  • Costs vary, and they stack on top. Ward can ask for mana, life, a discard, or a sacrifice. It is paid on top of the spell's own cost, not instead of it. (CR 702.21a)
  • X in the cost. Some ward abilities use X and state what it equals. The value is set when the ward trigger resolves, not when it triggers. (CR 702.21b)

What Gets Around Ward

  • Non-targeting effects. A board wipe, an edict, or anything that never uses the word target does not trigger ward at all, so there is nothing to pay. (CR 115.1a)
  • Spells that can't be countered. Ward still triggers, but if the opponent declines to pay, the counter has nothing to do and the spell resolves. Uncounterable removal ignores ward. (CR 701.5)
  • Countering the trigger. Ward is a triggered ability sitting on the stack, so an effect that counters that trigger lets the spell resolve with no payment. (CR 603.3)
  • Gaining ward too late. The permanent must already have ward when the spell targets it. Giving it ward in response does nothing, since the targeting already happened. (CR 702.21a)

Ward vs Hexproof and Shroud

  • Toll, not a wall. Hexproof and shroud forbid the targeting outright. Ward allows it and charges a cost, which is why a wrath or an edict still gets around all three. (CR 702.21a)
  • Multiple targets, multiple tolls. A spell that targets two ward permanents triggers ward twice. The opponent pays both or the whole spell is countered. (CR 702.21a)

Worked Scenarios

Four boards that show ward as a toll, not a wall. The first two are when it fails, the last two are when it bites.

Paying Through Ward

Your creature has ward {2}. Your opponent casts a removal spell targeting it. Ward triggers and goes on the stack above the removal, resolving first. They have two extra mana, so they pay it, the trigger resolves, and the removal then resolves and kills the creature. Ward cost them two mana and nothing more. (CR 702.21a, 701.5)

Declining Against an Uncounterable Spell

Your creature has ward {3}. Your opponent casts removal that can't be countered and targets it. Ward still triggers, but they choose not to pay. The counter has nothing to do, since the spell can't be countered, so it resolves and the creature dies. Ward gives no protection against spells that can't be countered. (CR 701.5)

Board Wipe Ignores Ward

Your creature has ward {4}. A board wipe gives all creatures -4/-4, or destroys all creatures. It never says target, so ward never triggers and there is nothing to pay. The creature dies with the rest of the board. Reach for non-targeting removal against ward. (CR 115.1a)

Two Targets, Two Payments

Your opponent's spell tries to target two of your creatures, each with ward {2}. Both ward abilities trigger. To resolve the spell the opponent has to pay four mana in total. If they only have two, both ward triggers still resolve and one goes unpaid, so the whole spell is countered, not just the half they couldn't cover. (CR 702.21a)
