Blood Moon

Enchantment

Nonbasic lands are Mountains.

Modern Legacy Vintage Commander Not Pioneer Not Standard
Current Oracle Text · From Gatherer

How It Works

Nonbasic Lands Under Blood Moon

Every nonbasic land on the battlefield becomes a Mountain. It loses its old land types and every ability from its own text, then gains only "tap for red." That ranges from a quiet loss of color to shutting a whole engine off.

Basics, names, and supertypes survive. Your basic Mountains and your opponents' basic lands are untouched. A converted land keeps its name and stays nonbasic, so Steam Vents can still be hit by nonbasic-land removal, and Dark Depths stays a legendary snow land, just one with no abilities.

Reading The Result

  • Every nonbasic taps for red and nothing else. A three or four color manabase becomes a stack of Mountains. The land keeps no mana ability except "tap for red," which is what strands a greedy deck off its colors.
  • Abilities vanish, identity stays. The land keeps its name and its supertypes. Dark Depths is still a legendary snow land and Glacial Chasm is still Glacial Chasm, but neither has any of its printed abilities, so the combo and the damage shield are both off.
  • Only lands on the battlefield change, and only while Blood Moon is there. A fetchland cracked in response still finds its land. Dark Depths keeps its ice counters but cannot remove them, so the moment Blood Moon leaves the lock can come undone.

Tips

✓ Aim it at greedy manabases

Blood Moon hits three and four color decks hardest. A deck leaning on duals, fetches, and utility lands can be stranded off its colors for several turns. Against a mono-color or basic-heavy opponent it does almost nothing, so read the table before you commit it.

✓ Keep your own base basic

Build with basic Mountains and a light splash so Blood Moon barely touches you while it wrecks the opponent. A red-heavy deck pays the smallest price, which is what turns a symmetrical effect into a one-sided one.

✓ Land it early

Three mana means it can come down turn two or three, before opponents have their colors set up. An early Blood Moon that catches a tapped-out hand of off-color lands is far more crippling than a late one played over an established board.

Misplays

✗ It does not make lands basic

Converted lands become Mountains but stay nonbasic. They still trip nonbasic-land hate like Fulminator Mage and Tectonic Edge, and they stay frozen under Back to Basics. Effects that reward basic lands do not switch on.

✗ Basics, names, and supertypes are safe

Your basic lands keep working, a legendary land stays legendary, and a converted land keeps its name. Nothing is renamed "Mountain," so a card that cares about a land's name still sees the original.

✗ It reaches only the battlefield

A nonbasic land in hand, in a library, or in a graveyard is not affected. A fetchland already in play is a useless Mountain, but one you sacrifice in response still searches for its land normally before Blood Moon takes hold.

Combos

Magus of the Moon Creature copy

Magus of the Moon is a 2/2 with the exact same "Nonbasic lands are Mountains" ability. Running both gives the deck more draws of the lock and a second angle that survives enchantment removal. Stacking the two does nothing extra, since the effect is identical, but the redundancy is the point.

Back to Basics Soft lock

Blood Moon never makes a land basic, so every converted land is still nonbasic. Back to Basics keys off nonbasic lands, so it still stops them from untapping. Together the opponent's lands are red-only and frozen, which can shut a multicolor deck out of the game.

Fine Print

  • Lands that would enter tapped now enter untapped. Blood Moon strips a land's abilities before it arrives, so an Arcane Sanctum or a Gruul Turf enters untapped, and you do not return a land for the bounce. Any "when this land enters" trigger does not happen either.
  • Urborg always loses to Blood Moon. Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth wants every land to be a Swamp, but Blood Moon removes that ability first, so every nonbasic land, Urborg included, is just a Mountain. The order they came down in does not matter.
  • Spreading Seas is decided by timestamp. Blood Moon and an effect like Spreading Seas both set a land's type, and neither one depends on the other, so whichever resolved later wins. The land is an Island if Spreading Seas landed after Blood Moon, a Mountain if Blood Moon landed after.
  • Your own lands are caught too. Blood Moon is symmetrical. It turns your nonbasic lands into Mountains as well, so build around it or expect to feel it yourself.

Official Rulings

2020-08-07

If a nonbasic land has an ability that triggers "when" it enters the battlefield, it will lose that ability before it can trigger.

2020-08-07

If a nonbasic land has an ability that causes it to enter the battlefield tapped, it will lose that ability before it can apply. The same is also true of any other abilities that modify how a land enters the battlefield or apply "as" a land enters the battlefield, such as the first ability of Cavern of Souls.

2020-08-07

Nonbasic lands will lose any other land types and abilities they had. They will gain the land type Mountain and gain the ability "{T}: Add {R}."

2020-08-07

This effect doesn't affect names or supertypes. It won't turn any land into a basic land or remove the legendary supertype from a legendary land, and the lands won't be named "Mountain."
