Privacy Policy

MTJawnny.com is a static, ad-free hobby site. Here's what actually happens when you visit it.

What we collect

Google Analytics 4 (GA4) runs on every page: aggregate stats like pages viewed, rough location, device type, and referrer. It's used to see what's getting used, not to identify you personally. GA4 sets cookies to do this; you can opt out with the Google Analytics Opt-out Browser Add-on, or block cookies in your own browser settings.

There are no accounts, no logins, and no server-side database on this site. It can't identify you by name or email, because it never asks for one anywhere on mtjawnny.com itself.

Third-party services this site links out to

  • Ko-fi (tipping): ko-fi.com/mtjawnny. Any payment or account info you give Ko-fi is handled entirely by Ko-fi, under Ko-fi's own privacy policy.
  • Tally (card-suggestion and bug-report forms): anything submitted through a "Suggest a card" or "Report a bug" link goes to Tally.so, not to this site, and is governed by Tally's privacy policy.
  • Instagram (@mtjawnny): an external social link.
  • Scryfall: some tools, like Token Grabber, query Scryfall's public API directly from your browser to look up card data. No personal info about you passes through this site to do that.
  • cdn.mtjawnny.com (Cloudflare R2): card art and mana symbols are served from this asset host, with a Scryfall-hosted fallback. It's a static CDN; it doesn't collect anything about you beyond standard server logs.
  • GitHub Pages: this site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which logs standard server-access data under GitHub's own privacy statement.

Cookies

The only cookies set on this site come from Google Analytics. There's no first-party tracking cookie, no ad-network pixel, and no cross-site retargeting.

Children's privacy

This site isn't directed at children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect information from them.

Using Proxies Ethically

This site frames proxies as playtesting, budget, and accessibility tools, never as counterfeits. If you print anything from mtjawnny.com, here's the line we ask you to hold:

  • Say what it is. A proxy is a stand-in, not a substitute for the real card anywhere that distinction matters. Don't pass a proxy off as an authentic card in a trade, a sale, or anything involving money changing hands.
  • Know your table, know your event. Casual playgroups and most Commander tables are proxy-friendly, but sanctioned tournaments, even many local ones, have their own rules about proxies, sometimes none at all. Check with your playgroup or tournament organizer before you sit down with proxies in your deck.
  • Never sell proxies as the real thing. Passing off a proxy as an authentic, tournament-legal Magic card, to anyone, for any price, isn't something this site supports or endorses.
  • The point is access, not deception. Proxies exist here so more people can try expensive cards, playtest a build before buying in, or play with cards they can't otherwise get, not to trick anyone.

If that's not how you're planning to use what you print, this site isn't for you.
